Kitchen Water Damage Help in San Jose

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help San Jose homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.
