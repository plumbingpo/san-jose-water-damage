Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across San Diego can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust.
